People are in the graph. A Practitioner holds a keyed phone hash (64 ASCII lowercase hex characters only), a bare email domain (ASCII only), and a staff display name. A staff name is normalised first (NFKD, then removal of format and combining characters, then NFKC). The phone lookup returns the active reporter roles a phone holds, minus those covered out of at `now`, plus those it covers until after `now`, sorted by service name, then service id, then role id. An ambiguous phone lookup is a Conflict. A naive `now` is refused before any query.

## Round 12: the precomposed postcode and the NFKD step
Probe: `Duty SW1Á 1AA` (`SW1Á 1AA` with the precomposed U+00C1), added as the case `postcode-with-precomposed-accent` in `test_hidden_digits_and_postcodes_are_refused_after_normalisation`, `tests/unit/domain/model/test_people_round11.py`.

Why it depends on the NFKD step: NFKC composes `A` and a combining acute into `Á`, which is then no longer a combining mark. A precomposed `Á` is never a combining mark in the first place. Only the NFKD step, which decomposes `Á` into `A` and U+0301 before the removal, lets the removal see the accent. Without it, the text keeps `Á`, the postcode pattern (`[A-Z]`) does not match, and the name is accepted.
