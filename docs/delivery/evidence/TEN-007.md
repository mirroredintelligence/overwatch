## Summary
The platform now has a record of its tenants that code can ask before any tenant is bound. `Tenant` and `TenantNumber` registry nodes, a read-only `TenantDirectory` over them (by id, by number called and Twilio account together, by account, by host, by Entra directory, the active tenants and their ids), seven global statements that join the allowlist, each pinned by the SHA-256 of its exact text (a global statement registers only when its id is pinned and its text matches, so the allowlist is not a way round the `$tenant` rule), with structural checks behind the pin (read-only, registry labels only, no keyword-hidden node pattern) as defence in depth, secret references that name only a tenant's own secret, a recording fake secret store, `tenants` and `secrets` on the container, a start-up that applies the migrations and makes sure the audit head exists, and the outbox relay reading its tenants from the directory instead of `DIUCS_RELAY_TENANTS`.
