# TEN-077: network administration use cases: network, members, agreement, listings (W9A)

Back end: `application/use_cases/network_admin.py` (`GovernanceRecorder`, `CreateNetwork`, `RecordAgreement`, `QueryNetworkAdmin`), `membership.py` (`InviteMember`, `AcceptMembership`, `LeaveNetwork`, `RemoveMember`, `LeaveAllNetworks`), `listing_admission.py` (`RequestListing`, `DecideListing`, `QueryListings`), `bootstrap/lifecycle_operations.py` (`network-create`), `scripts/tenant_lifecycle.py`.

## Decisions

- DEC-960 The registry decides, the records follow, and every step is repeatable (a departure from the plan's "registry write last"). The registry is the one place two administrators can race, so its compare-and-set comes first: it decides who won, and nothing is recorded for a write that lost (a record of an approval that did not happen would be a false audit). The records then follow in the two scopes that did not write it (`GovernanceRecorder`): the acting tenant's chain with the events on its own outbox (the relay publishes them to `network.events` under the acting tenant), then the network's chain; two units, never nested, each guarded by a processed marker keyed by the action (`ApproveGrant:<grant>:<party>`, `EndMembership:<membership>`, `AdmitListing:<listing>:<decided-at>`), so a step that failed after the registry write is finished by the same command run again and nothing is recorded twice. Creating a network is the one saga that starts elsewhere: its audit chain head comes first (the known gap of DEC-543 item 7: a network scope had no head), then the registry record, the owner's membership (id `mem-<network>-owner`), the records. An action that creates a thing nobody has yet recorded is **compensated** when its records fail (an invitation is removed, a listing is withdrawn, a grant proposal is declined) so no orphan waits; an action that ends something is never compensated (closing is final) and is finished by retry. Ending closes the gate first (the registry), then the network scope, then the records. [Departure] The plan said "registry write last"; for an action that makes something effective the failure it protects against (sharing before it is recorded) is reduced by the retry and by the heartbeat, not removed: see the known issue.
- DEC-961 Who does what. The **platform operator** creates a network for an active owner (`CreateNetwork`, a lifecycle step like creating a tenant; the script command `network-create`; not a route). The owner's **`network-admin`** runs the sharing of the network (the agreement, invitations, removal, the listing decisions, the owner's approval of a grant); a member's **`tenant-admin`** runs that organisation's side (accepting an invitation and the agreement version it read, leaving, its grants, withdrawing a listing); a **`lead`** may also ask for and withdraw a listing. A role that may not do a thing is `NotAllowed` before any read; a network, membership, grant or listing that is not the actor's own, or does not exist, is the one `NotFound` (so a network id, who owns it and who belongs to it cannot be discovered); an invitation to an organisation that is unknown and one that is not active are the same `NotFound`. The reference to the person who acted (an approval, an ending) is the token's subject (`stf-` and 32 hex digits, the person at that tenant): it must be named and of the shape the records accept (`account_ref_of`). The owner can neither leave nor be removed (the domain's rule); the removal of a member needs one network administrator and a reason code (the audit's "second administrator approves" is not built: known issue). A member's accepting names the agreement version it read and must equal the network's current one; a new version marks every member `agreement_behind` and changes nothing that is shared (DEC-132). An invitation that was withdrawn is, to the invitee, not found.
- DEC-965 A listing is the permission to project a service, nothing more, and its id is letters only. The owner reads each listing by the organisation's name and the member's own service id (the listing carries no service name: known issue); no read model holds a tenant id or an account reference. The listing's id is the network board's row reference (DEC-546) and the board refuses an id that the privacy guard reads as personal data; a random id can hold a postcode shape by chance (`b59al`), which would make one listing fail the whole board. `RequestListing` therefore makes the id of letters only (`lst-` and the generated id with digits turned into letters, separators into one letter each: two different inputs stay different), pinned by a test with awkward generators. Suspending, declining, reinstating and withdrawing are compare-and-sets on the status the decider saw; the same decision repeated by the same person finishes its records and changes nothing; its record key holds the instant of the decision, so a second suspension months later is its own record. A listing change reaches the network board within the cache backstop (60 s) and the producer within the heartbeat interval (5 minutes): there is no listing event.
- DEC-966 below is TEN-078's.

## Known issues

| id | what | where | trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| TEN-077 | An action that makes sharing effective writes the registry first, so between that write and its records (a failure, then no retry) sharing is effective without its record; the retry and the heartbeat's reconciliation do not detect a missing record | `network_admin.py` | a process that dies between the registry write and the records, and a client that does not retry |
| TEN-077 | The owner sees a pending listing as the organisation and the service's id only; the owner needs the service's name to decide, and a listing has none (the projection that carries it exists only after admission) | `listing_admission.py`, registry `Listing` | the first listings of a real network |
| TEN-077 | The network's audit chain cannot be read or verified through the audit port (`_chain_tenant` refuses a network scope: its entries have no one tenant to be parsed under); the integration rig verifies it link by link | `audit_log.py` | an audit reader for a network |
| TEN-077 | The person recorded on an approval is the token's subject at that tenant, so one person who holds a role at two tenants has two references; "two different accounts" holds within a tenant and between two tenants by construction, but not for one person across both | `account_ref_of` | an owner that is also the grantor with the same person as both approvers (needs the cross-tenant account reference in the token, DEC-524) |
| TEN-077 | Removing a member needs one network administrator (the audit proposed a second approver) | `membership.py` | a removal without a second pair of eyes |
| TEN-077 | Listing admission does not check the ODS code or name for a duplicate of another member's service (hazard H-N12's second control) | `listing_admission.py` | two members listing one real service |
| TEN-077 | A tenant being offboarded stops being relayed once its record moves, so any `MemberRemoved` of a membership that ended after the move is not published (the network board then refetches within its 60 s backstop); the offboarding ends memberships before the move to avoid it | `tenant_offboard.py` | a membership ended after the record moved |
