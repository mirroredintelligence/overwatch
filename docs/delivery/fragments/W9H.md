# W9H: the executive in the reporter app (W7C-K5), and the district chips of the map (W50)

Bundle W9H, branch `task/build-W9H` off `integration/build` 178b50b. Numbers: DEC-1040 to DEC-1049, H-710 to H-719. Two items, back end and front end each.

## Decisions

### DEC-1040 What the executive needs from the reporter app is its notifications and nothing else; an executive holds no reporter post (W7C-K5, W39, W40, W8E)
Status: accepted (wave 9, bundle W9H; under DEC-133, no owner gate). **This departs from the brief's reading** ("the exec reports or confirms capacity for services they hold a post for"), for these reasons, each checked in the code:

1. An executive is staff. They sign in through the organisation's directory (`ExchangeStaffToken`), and the token's subject is a derived staff subject (`stf-` and hex), with no services, no device, no phone and no PIN. A reporter post (`PractitionerRole`, duty manager or coordinator) belongs to a `Practitioner` that is found by the keyed hash of a phone number. Nothing links the two, and nothing should: linking a directory identity to a phone-held person would let a directory group mapping (an administrator's decision) reach reporting rights, which are granted by a lead to a number. DEC-136 item 7's account reference links a person across tenants, not a staff identity to a phone.
2. The PIN session (`session_role`, `ReporterSignIn`) gives role `reporter` to anyone who holds a reporter post, whatever else they are. So an executive who also runs a service signs in to the reporter app as a reporter, with their own phone and PIN, and reports and confirms as a reporter. That needs no admission and no change; it is two identities, as DEC-134 item 4 keeps them. The W8E rule stands: the token the BFF exchanges for a PIN session is for role reporter only (DEC-941).
3. The routes that read or write a post (`/reporter/services`, prompt, report, history, batch, settings, cover, channel, accept, set-up summary, bookings) have no meaning for an executive: they would answer an empty list or a 404 for a staff subject. They stay closed to the executive, so the attack surface is not widened for nothing.

What the executive does need from the installed app is what W39 and W34 give them: the alerts they chose (W39) delivered to their phone. That is: save a Web Push subscription, ask for a test notification, and have the alert pushes (K3) reach them. So the executive is admitted to exactly three back-end routes: `POST /identity/push-subscription` and `DELETE /identity/push-subscription` (already admitted by role, but unusable: see DEC-1042) and `POST /reporter/push-test` (DEC-1043). Every other `/reporter/...` route refuses an executive with the 404 of every refused role (DEC-086); the role matrix test covers all thirteen roles on all fourteen reporter routes and the three notification routes.

### DEC-1041 A PIN session and a code sign-in make a token for role reporter only (W8E, DEC-940, DEC-941)
Status: accepted. `SESSION_ROLES` in `registration_session.py` was `{reporter, exec}`; it is `{reporter}`. A session that names any other role is one nobody derived (`session_role` never makes one), and the exchange answers the one 404 of a session that cannot be exchanged, the same as an unknown or ended one. Test: all twelve other roles, one answer.

### DEC-1042 An executive's person record is made on their first push save (EnsureStaffPerson)
Status: accepted. `Q-PUSH-SAVE` matches a `Practitioner`, and a staff subject has none, so an executive's subscription could not be saved (`CheckReporterAccess` refused them first: 401). `EnsureStaffPerson` (`use_cases/staff_person.py`) makes the minimal record on the first save, in the bound tenant only: id the subject, active, display name `Executive` (a word, no directory name is copied), no phone, no email. A record that exists is left exactly as it is, so a person who was removed is not brought back by signing in again, and `CheckReporterAccess` then refuses them (401) as it does any removed person. Only role `exec`; another role gets `NotAllowed`. One audit entry (`EnsureStaffPerson`, the subject). Closes U33's "the exec role is not represented on a practitioner" for the push path only; the lead is not covered (known issue).

### DEC-1043 The executive's test notification is a person push; the BFF takes the executive on the push routes with their own wording and a device derived from the endpoint
Status: accepted. `SendPushTest` for role `exec` queues one outbox message of the same kind alerts use (K3, `recipient_practitioner_id`, plain title and body: "Test notification" and "Alerts are working on this phone.", no link, no service, no figure, no person), with an audit entry. No Communication is written, because there is no post for one to be about. No saved subscription is `NoPushSubscription` (409 `no-subscription`); three tests in ten minutes per person as for a reporter. The BFF: `POST /api/bff/push/subscriptions` and `/test` take roles `reporter` and `exec`. An executive's session has no device, so the device their subscription is kept under is the SHA-256 of the subscription's own endpoint (43 base64url characters, never sent by the page, a stable id for that browser; the same browser saving again replaces its own subscription). The consent wording is `exec-v1` for an executive and `v1` for a reporter, and each role is refused the other's.

### DEC-1044 The push sender delivers a person push (K3 with `recipient_practitioner_id`), which alerts need and never had (H-710)
Status: accepted. `PushMessageSender` accepted only a Communication addressed to a post's role, with sealed content. The alert push (`alerts._push`, built in U34) writes a payload with `recipient_practitioner_id`, `title` and `body` and no communication or sealed content, so every alert push to an executive or a lead failed `parse_outbound` with `BAD_PAYLOAD`, a permanent error, and was dead-lettered: **no executive alert has ever reached a phone**. The sender now recognises a payload that names a person: channel push, catalogue id K3, title at most 120 and body at most 300 characters and not blank, passed through `render(K3)` (the same patient-data and link checks), and no `recipient_role_id` (one recipient kind per message). It sends to every installed app of that person at high urgency, deletes a subscription the provider reports gone, retries the whole message on a transient failure, and logs counts only. No Communication is settled (there is none). The text is a service's name and the kind of alert (or the test words), held in the outbox payload in clear, as it is today; it names no patient (the K3 template and `_refuse_patient_data` guard that), and carries no link.

### DEC-1045 The districts of a not-found or out-of-area postcode come with `$filter-options`; a district alone is kept only through its own field (W50, W8A-K6, W50-K2, DEC-647)
Status: accepted. Superseded part: DEC-647 ("the district chips are not built: the back end stores the postcode district only and the BFF's save needs a full postcode").
1. **The offer.** `FilterOptionsResult.districts` and `district` Parameters parts (`valueString`, one each, in the order to show them), sent only when `notFound` or `outOfArea` is true, and none otherwise. They are the districts of the tenant's current criteria (the read `FilterOptions` already makes, so no new graph statement and no new tenant-pending), of services that are not suspended, that the locator can place (a district it cannot place would lead back to the same state). The case's own district is never offered back. Nothing in the list is about a case or a person: it is the services' configuration.
2. **The choice.** `UpdateCaseCommand.district` and the extension `https://diucs.example/StructureDefinition/postcode-district` (`valueCode`) on the contained Patient of a PUT. `apply_update` takes it through `normalise_district` (upper case, trimmed; `DT4 8XX`, `DT48X9`, full-width and a list are refused, and the refusal does not echo the value), refuses it together with a full `postcode` in one body, and keeps it as the case's district. `postcode` still takes a full postcode and nothing less: a district typed into the needs form is still refused. The district is part of the command fingerprint. A closed case is a 409.
3. **The BFF** `PUT /api/bff/cases/[ref]/district` takes `{district}` and nothing else (a body with another field, or a district that is not `^[A-Z]{1,2}[0-9][A-Z0-9]?$`, is a 400 before the back end), reads the case for its ETag, saves with the ETag and the screen's key, and answers the case view. Advisor only, as every advisor route (DEC-860); the back end admits advisor and clinical.
4. **The screens.** The not-found and out-of-area states (map and list) show the districts as buttons in a group named "Choose the district", the spec's note "Options will use the district, with a wider travel range.", and keep the other ways on (ask a clinical advisor, other routes under local protocol, change the postcode). Pressing one saves it, says "Using DT3.", and reads the options again; a choice that cannot be saved says "We could not use that district. Try again, or choose another." and offers the chips again. With no district to choose from the sentence is "Check it with the caller." (it does not point at chips that are not there).

### DEC-1046 The order and the number of chips; the list shows the postcode state only when nothing is left to show (W50)
Status: accepted. `domain/rules/district_choice.py`. With a place for the patient (the district is known but covered by no service) the nearest district first (flat-earth distance, ties by name). With none (the district is not known) the districts of the case's own postcode area first (DT before BH), then the rest, each in natural order (DT2 before DT10). At most twelve [ASSUMED: enough for the neighbours of one district, few enough to read on a phone]. On the list (the primary view) the postcode state replaces "No service fits right now" only when there are no options and the back end says the postcode was not found or is out of area; a postcode not found with options on offer (a covered district the locator cannot place: no travel times) keeps the list. The list does not read the case, so its out-of-area sentence starts "This postcode"; the map keeps the district it read.

### DEC-1047 The installed app's front door and the executive's way in (W39, W34)
Status: accepted. The manifest's start address is `/app`. A signed-in executive (a directory session) opening `/app` goes to `/planning`, not to the PIN screen they cannot use; the PIN screen offers "Sign in with your organisation account" (`/sign-in?next=/planning`) for an executive whose session ended. The executive's set-up ends on `/planning/phone` ("Alerts on this phone": what we send, what we never send, who chooses, what is recorded; Allow, then the browser's prompt, the public key from the back end, the subscription saved with `exec-v1`; a test button; links to the alert settings and the home screen), where W39 pointed at the reporter's home-screen step, whose copy and steps are the reporter's. A lead, who shares `/planning`, is sent from that page to the home screen: leads are not admitted to the notification routes (known issue), and a button that fails is worse than none. The executive home links to it ("Alerts on this phone"). `lib/auth/access.ts` needed no change: `/planning` is already `exec` and `lead`; the page decides for the lead.
