**The product.** DIUCS capacity helps an NHS urgent-care single point of access (SPOA) in Dorset. Community services, wards and virtual wards report how many referrals or beds they can take, by text message, a one-tap link, or an installed web app. Call advisors see a live wall board and a filtered list of services that fit a patient's needs, then ask a service to take the patient by text and wait for CONFIRMED. **The architecture.** Hexagonal. Dependencies point inwards only. A Next.js front end in `frontend/` talks only to its own `/api/bff/*` route handlers, which call the back end's FHIR R4B API. Neo4j is the only database, Redis is a cache, Azure Service Bus carries messages and scheduled work.

**Goal.** Give the advisor's options a patient position and a travel range without sending patient postcodes outside the Trust: a local postcode-centroid lookup, and an interim straight-line travel estimate until a routing engine is chosen. These are external-data adapters with no Neo4j involvement.

**Read first.**
- `backend/src/diucs/application/ports/outbound/geo.py` (P11): `PostcodeLocator`, `TravelTime`, `LatLon`, `TravelRange`.
- `backend/src/diucs/adapters/testing/fixed_geo.py` (P11): the fakes use-case tests keep using.
- `backend/src/diucs/domain/rules/postcode.py` (D19): `normalise_postcode`, `postcode_district`.
- `docs/spec/architecture.md`: "F8. Position and travel time" and the Geocoding row; `docs/spec/ui-screens.md` artboard "MapWeb" ("A range, not a promise").

**Rules you need.**
- Patient postcodes never leave the Trust's hosting; the centroid file is local. **Open decision:** the dataset (ONS Postcode Directory or OS Code-Point Open) and its licence for NHS use. Accept a CSV with columns `postcode,latitude,longitude` (WGS84); converting the source is a one-off script, out of scope. Ship a small fixture CSV with made-up DT postcodes.
- Normalise first. Unknown full postcode → the district centroid (mean of that district's postcodes) with `approximate=True`; unknown district → `None`. A partial postcode such as `DT4 8X` resolves at district level.
- **Open decision: routing engine.** Until it is chosen (I15), `StraightLineTravelTime` estimates road time from great-circle distance: road distance = straight × 1.3, speeds 50 km/h (low) and 30 km/h (high) **[ASSUMED]**, rounded to 5 minutes, minimum 5, `source = "straight-line estimate"`. Named constants. Always a range.
- Visiting services: their travel is part of their response time; the use case decides that, not this adapter.

**Build.**
- `backend/src/diucs/adapters/outbound/postcodes_local/centroids.py`: `CsvPostcodeLocator(path)` implementing `PostcodeLocator`, loaded once; `centroid(postcode) -> LatLon | None` and `locate(postcode) -> Located(lat_lon, approximate) | None`.
- `backend/src/diucs/adapters/outbound/travel_time/straight_line.py`: `StraightLineTravelTime`; pure `haversine_km(a, b)`.
- Setting `postcode_centroids_path`; fixture `backend/tests/fixtures/postcode_centroids_sample.csv`. Wire both into the production profile of the container; the test profile keeps the `Fixed*` fakes.

**Tests to write first.** Unit tests, no Docker.
1. `centroid("dt4 8xx")` returns the fixture coordinates for `DT4 8XX`.
2. Unknown full postcode in a known district → district mean, `approximate=True`.
3. `DT4 8X` resolves at district level; `ZZ9 9ZZ` → `None`.
4. `haversine_km` between two known points matches a reference within 0.1 km.
5. A 20 km straight line → 26 km road → low 30 min, high 55 min.
6. Very short distances give at least 5 minutes; `source` is `"straight-line estimate"`.
7. The CSV is opened once (spy).
