**The product.** DIUCS capacity helps NHS urgent-care coordination services see, in real time, which community services, wards and virtual wards can take a patient. Services report how many referrals or beds they can take, by text message, a one-tap link, or an installed web app. Call advisors see a live wall board and a filtered list of services that fit a patient's needs, then ask a service to take the patient by text and wait for CONFIRMED. Shift leads chase late reports, register services and keep criteria current. Nothing anywhere holds a patient name or NHS number. The platform is **multi-tenant** (DEC-130, DEC-131, DEC-132): each organisation is a tenant, the legal entity and data controller for its own data; organisations collaborate in a network only through grants and read-only projections. The first deployment is the "default tenant": what its people read must not change. This task belongs to the tenancy retrofit (`docs/delivery/tenancy-plan.md`, section 2).

**The architecture.** Hexagonal. A Python back end (package `diucs` in `backend/src/diucs`) with four layers: `domain` (pure rules, no I/O), `application` (use cases and ports defined as `typing.Protocol`), `adapters` (inbound: FHIR REST, SMS webhook, link pages, workers; outbound: Neo4j, Redis, Azure Service Bus, SMS, email, push; `adapters/testing`: fakes for external providers only) and `bootstrap` (settings and the composition root). Dependencies point inwards only. A Next.js front end in `frontend/` talks only to its own `/api/bff/*` route handlers, which call the back end's FHIR R4B API. Neo4j is the only database, Redis is a cache, Azure Service Bus carries messages and scheduled work.
