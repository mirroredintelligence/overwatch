**The product.** DIUCS capacity helps an NHS urgent-care single point of access (SPOA) in Dorset. Community services, wards and virtual wards report how many referrals or beds they can take, by text message, a one-tap link, or an installed web app. Call advisors see a live wall board and a filtered list of services that fit a patient's needs, then ask a service to take the patient by text and wait for CONFIRMED. Shift leads chase late reports, register services and keep criteria current. Nothing anywhere holds a patient name or NHS number.

**The architecture.** Hexagonal. A Python back end (package `diucs` in `backend/src/diucs`) with four layers: `domain` (pure rules, no I/O), `application` (use cases and ports defined as `typing.Protocol`), `adapters` (inbound: FHIR REST, SMS webhook, link pages, workers; outbound: Neo4j, Redis, Azure Service Bus, SMS, email, push; `adapters/testing`: fakes for external providers only) and `bootstrap` (settings and the composition root). Dependencies point inwards only. A Next.js front end in `frontend/` talks only to its own `/api/bff/*` route handlers, which call the back end's FHIR R4B API. Neo4j is the only database, Redis is a cache, Azure Service Bus carries messages and scheduled work.

**Rules you need.**
- The invitation text links to `/i/<token>`. That page looks the token up and redirects to `/setup?invite=<token>`; an expired or used invitation shows "This invitation has expired. Ask the SPOA shift lead to send a new one." Invitations last 48 hours.
- Welcome: "Set up capacity reporting for <service>" with the five steps and times (Check your number 30 seconds; Choose how we contact you 1 minute; Add to home screen 1 minute, app only; Allow notifications App only; Create a PIN 30 seconds), "You can stop and come back to the link within 48 hours.", button **Start**.
- Check number ("Step 1 of 5"): show the masked number from the invitation ("07xxx xxx456"), never the full number; "Not your number? Ask the SPOA shift lead to send a new invitation."; **Send code**.
- Enter code: six single-digit boxes with one accessible label "6-digit code" (pasting 6 digits fills all boxes; `inputmode="numeric"`, `autocomplete="one-time-code"`); "Send a new code in 0:42" counting down from 60 seconds, then a **Send a new code** button; "The code works for 10 minutes. After 5 wrong tries you must ask for a new code."; **Check code**. Wrong code: "That code is not right. You have N tries left." After 5: "Too many tries. Send a new code."
- Choose how ("Step 2 of 5"): App notification ("Recommended. A notification opens the app. Needs adding to your home screen."), Text message ("A text with a link. Works on any phone."), Email ("An email with a link."); "You can change this later in Settings."; **Continue**. Text or Email posts the choice and goes to `/setup/done?channel=text|email`, a short page "You are set up. We will contact you at your usual time." App goes to `/setup/home-screen` (built by W34).
- The same flow serves COO and ICB users when the invitation role is `exec`: skip the channel step and go straight to `/setup/home-screen` (W39 adds their alert choices).
- BFF: `GET /api/bff/invitations/[token]` → `{service, role, maskedPhone, expiresAt, state:"valid"|"expired"|"used"}`; `POST /api/bff/identity/code {invite}` → `{resendAfterSeconds}`; `POST /api/bff/identity/code/verify {invite, code}` → `{ok:true}` (sets the session cookie) or `{ok:false, triesLeft}`; `POST /api/bff/reporter/channel {channel}`. CSRF on every POST.
- data-el: `ONB-WELCOME-START-BTN`, `ONB-CODE-SEND-BTN`, `ONB-CODE-CHECK-BTN`, `ONB-CODE-RESEND-BTN`, `ONB-CHANNEL-<APP|TEXT|EMAIL>`, `ONB-CHANNEL-CONTINUE-BTN`.
